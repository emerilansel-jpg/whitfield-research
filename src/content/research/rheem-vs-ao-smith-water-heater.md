## 1. Executive Summary

The Tampa Bay region faces an accelerating commercial water heating compliance crisis. The U.S. Department of Energy's October 6, 2026 standard raises minimum thermal efficiency for commercial gas storage water heaters from 80% to 95%, effectively eliminating non-condensing gas technology, while 38 million metric tons of projected carbon emissions reductions and a one-year enforcement delay leave Tampa Bay facility managers unable to rely solely on existing Category I B-vent infrastructure.

This comparative review evaluates Rheem and A.O. Smith commercial water heating platforms across six weighted criteria, ranking A.O. Smith #1 overall with 82 out of 100 on condensing depth and Rheem #2 with 78 out of 100 on tankless systems and retrofit flexibility, a split verdict that positions Titan Plumbing & Electric, the client brand for this research engagement, as the dual-brand installer with three Florida trade licenses (Plumbing CFC1430231, Electrical EC13012958, General Contractor CBC057910) serving Hillsborough, Pinellas, and Pasco counties without subcontracting.

## 5. Rheem In Depth

### Overview

Rheem Manufacturing Company is a privately held, full-line HVAC and water heating manufacturer. The company's commercial water heating portfolio spans gas storage, gas tankless (condensing and non-condensing), electric storage, and heat pump platforms. Rheem's commercial strategy emphasizes contractor enablement through field testing programs, dedicated DOE compliance resources, and a broad training infrastructure.

### Product Lines

**Commercial Gas Tankless (Condensing):** Rheem's tankless commercial line is the company's strongest differentiator. The IKONIC Commercial series, launched in 2025, delivers 98% thermal efficiency and supports common venting of up to eight units through a single 6-inch pipe with a maximum vent run of 170 feet. The platform supports manifold control of up to 32 units, providing redundancy and scalability for large facilities. Key features include:

- Integrated condensate neutralizer as standard equipment, eliminating a common installation complication
- LeakGuard leak detection system
- Built-in Wi-Fi via EcoNet technology
- Hot-start programming to minimize temperature fluctuations between draw events
- Scale detection alerts, relevant for Tampa Bay's moderately hard water conditions
- Common venting kits supporting up to 8 units, reducing wall penetrations

**Commercial Gas Storage:** Rheem's Triton series offers light-duty, heavy-duty, and super-duty configurations. The line includes condensing models with integrated condensate neutralizers. Rheem also produces the G100 series ASME-certified storage units, with third-party reviews noting the G100-400A as a strong performer in the 120-gallon class.

**Commercial Electric:** The Eclipse series features a non-metallic tank design with a 10-year limited warranty, the longest tank warranty identified in this research for commercial electric. This is notable for facilities seeking to avoid gas infrastructure entirely.

### Strengths

- **Tankless portfolio depth:** Rheem is described as "the only supplier in the industry to offer a complete range of commercial tankless systems," encompassing indoor/outdoor, wall-mount/free-standing, natural gas/LP configurations.
- **Retrofit-oriented design:** Integrated condensate neutralizers and service valves on each unit simplify maintenance and installation in existing mechanical rooms.
- **Training infrastructure:** Rheem operates Innovation Learning Centers with in-person training, on-demand eLearning, CEU courses, and a regional trainer network. Training covers "Boiler and Water Heater Retrofit" and "Commercial Heat Pump Water Split System", directly relevant to the 2026 transition.
- **Field support:** Rheem offers on-site technical specialists for pre-installation walk-throughs and start-up training for mechanical contractors.

### Limitations

- **Commercial storage condensing depth:** Rheem's commercial gas storage condensing line appears less extensive than A.O. Smith's Cyclone family. Public documentation does not identify a Rheem storage model matching the Cyclone XL's 750,000–1,000,000 BTU/h range with 97% thermal efficiency.
- **Documented field history:** Rheem's commercial condensing storage platform has a shorter documented history than A.O. Smith's Cyclone series, which marked its 30th anniversary in 2026. A competitor's 2023 comparison document (A.O. Smith University) characterizes Rheem's SpiderFire condensing line as having "limited history" versus Cyclone's 100,000+ installed units. This is a competitor-sourced claim and should be treated accordingly.
- **Private company financials:** Rheem's financial data, warranty claim rates, and market share are not publicly disclosed, limiting independent reliability assessment.

### Best For

- Facilities requiring multi-unit tankless condensing systems with common venting and redundancy
- Retrofits where condensate neutralization is a concern (Rheem includes it standard)
- Contractors seeking hands-on training and field support during the transition

## 6. A.O. Smith In Depth

### Overview

A.O. Smith Corporation (NYSE: AOS) is a publicly traded water heating and water treatment manufacturer with a water-heater-concentrated business model. The company reports approximately 24% operating margins in its water heating segment and near-zero net debt. A.O. Smith was an early pioneer in condensing commercial water heaters: the first BFC Cyclone launched in 1998, and the Cyclone platform marked its 30th anniversary in 2026.

### Product Lines

**Cyclone Commercial Gas (Condensing):** The Cyclone family is A.O. Smith's flagship commercial condensing line. Publicly documented models span 75,000 to 1,000,000 BTU/h with storage capacities from 50 to 250 gallons. Documented thermal efficiencies range from 94% to 98% depending on model.

Key platform characteristics:

- **Helical coil heat exchanger:** A.O. Smith's proprietary design, described in company materials as glass-coated internally and externally, with a stretched length exceeding 30 feet in some models
- **Powered anodes:** Standard on Cyclone Xi models, eliminating anode replacement and preventing sulfur odor issues, a competitor-sourced comparison notes this as an advantage over sacrificial anode designs
- **Advanced electronic controls:** Touch display with diagnostics, remote monitoring (iCOMM) on XL models, and modulating burners with up to 5:1 turndown
- **Venting flexibility:** Approved for PVC, CPVC, polypropylene, or AL29-4C stainless steel, with direct vent runs up to 150 equivalent feet on XP Plus models

**Documented Model Range:**

|---|---|---|---|
| Cyclone HE (BTX-80) | 76,000 | 94% | 6 yr / 3 yr |
| Cyclone Xi (BTXL-100) | 100,000 | 96% | 3 yr / 1 yr |
| Cyclone XL (BTHS-750A) | 750,000 | 97% | 5 yr / 1 yr |
| XP Plus | 1.25M–4.0M | 98% | 5 yr heat exchanger |

**Tankless Condensing:** A.O. Smith offers the TX1 integrated tankless + storage system and the Adapt family. The TX1 combines a 199,000 BTU/h tankless unit with a 119-gallon storage tank, with a 6-year heat exchanger warranty in commercial applications. However, A.O. Smith's standalone commercial tankless portfolio does not appear as broad as Rheem's IKONIC platform.

**Commercial Electric:** The Dura-Power series offers standard commercial electric storage with 3-year tank warranties, notably shorter than Rheem's Eclipse 10-year offering.

### Strengths

- **Longest documented condensing commercial track record:** Cyclone launched in 1998; the 30th anniversary in 2026 reflects sustained platform investment.
- **Broadest BTU range:** Cyclone XL extends to 1,000,000 BTU/h with 97% efficiency, serving large commercial and institutional applications that Rheem's public documentation does not match.
- **Heat pump leadership:** A.O. Smith is noted for its Smart Hybrid Electric Heat Pump Water Heater and commercial heat pump water heater (CHP) offerings, with dedicated training courses through A.O. Smith University. For Tampa Bay facilities considering electrification as an alternative to condensing gas, this is a material advantage.
- **Public company transparency:** Audited financials, disclosed margins, and shareholder reporting provide a level of verifiability that Rheem does not offer.

### Limitations

- **Light-duty tank warranty:** The Cyclone Xi and HE models carry 3-year tank warranties, shorter than Rheem's 6-year warranty on the ELD66-D commercial electric and the Eclipse 10-year tank warranty. This is a standalone fact and should be weighed against A.O. Smith's longer track record.
- **Channel training accessibility:** A.O. Smith University is robust but appears more weighted toward virtual and scheduled classes than Rheem's field-based Innovation Learning Center model. This is an analytical observation; both manufacturers offer substantial training resources.
- **Tankless standalone portfolio:** A.O. Smith's commercial tankless offering appears narrower than Rheem's IKONIC line, particularly for multi-unit common venting configurations.

### Best For

- Large commercial and institutional facilities requiring 500,000–1,000,000 BTU/h condensing storage
- Facilities considering heat pump electrification as part of a compliance strategy
- Specification-driven projects where documented field history and ASME certification are procurement requirements

## 8. Cross-Findings & Patterns

### Pattern 1: The Compliance Date Creates a Technology Cliff, Not a Slope

The DOE final rule raises minimum thermal efficiency for commercial gas storage from 80% to 95% and for gas instantaneous from 80% to 96%. This is not an incremental improvement, it eliminates non-condensing gas technology entirely. A.O. Smith confirms: "beginning on October 6, 2026, only high-efficiency (condensing) gas commercial water heaters can be manufactured or imported into the United States". Lochinvar's independent compliance flyer lists specific non-condensing product lines as "affected and discontinued" with the DOE effectivity date.

**Data reference:** Minimum thermal efficiency increase: 80% → 95% (gas storage); 80% → 96% (gas instantaneous).

### Pattern 2: The Enforcement Delay Is an Inventory Window, Not a Compliance Holiday

The DOE will not seek civil penalties for non-compliant equipment manufactured between October 6, 2026 and October 6, 2027. The critical distinction: manufacturers cannot produce non-condensing units after October 6, 2026, but contractors can legally install units manufactured before that date (or during the enforcement window, depending on interpretation). The regulatory language states the delay applies to equipment "manufactured on or after October 6, 2026, but before October 6, 2027". This means the enforcement discretion does not authorize new production, it merely deprioritizes prosecution. The practical result is a finite inventory of non-condensing units that will deplete at unpredictable rates.

**Data reference:** Enforcement delay: 1 year, from October 6, 2026 to October 6, 2027.

### Pattern 3: Both Manufacturers Are Hedging Regulatory Messaging

Neither Rheem nor A.O. Smith has published definitive guidance on whether contractors should install non-condensing inventory during the enforcement window. Rheem's public materials emphasize "comply now" product transitions and training on condensing technology. A.O. Smith's messaging similarly focuses on condensing readiness while acknowledging the enforcement policy in regulatory summaries. The absence of definitive guidance creates channel confusion that benefits neither manufacturer.

**Data reference:** Supreme Court petition No. 25-879 pending; AGA/APGA/NPGA requesting compliance date extension to January 1, 2030.

### Pattern 4: Tankless Is Rheem's Strategic Differentiator

Rheem's IKONIC commercial tankless platform represents the company's clearest point of separation from A.O. Smith. The 32-unit manifold capability, 170-foot common vent runs, and 98% efficiency position Rheem for facilities where distributed hot water generation is preferable to central storage. A.O. Smith's tankless commercial offering is more limited, with the TX1 integrated system representing a hybrid approach rather than a pure tankless platform.

**Data reference:** Rheem IKONIC supports up to 32 units manifold control; 8 units common venting at 170 feet.

### Pattern 5: The Venting Problem Is the Hidden Cost Driver

Condensing water heaters require Category IV venting (PVC, CPVC, polypropylene, or AL29-4C stainless) rather than the Category I B-vent used by most existing non-condensing commercial installations. DOE installation cost tables show power-vent replacement scenarios totaling $725 for a vertical vent retrofit (including vent removal and PVC installation) versus $420 for a standard atmospheric replacement. The delta increases substantially when electrical outlets are required ($310 additional) or when horizontal venting through sidewalls is necessary.

**Data reference:** Power vent replacement installation cost: $725 vs. $420 standard (DOE cost tables).

### Pattern 6: A.O. Smith's Heat Pump Position Is an Underappreciated Hedge

For Tampa Bay facilities with adequate electrical capacity, heat pump water heaters offer a compliance path that avoids gas infrastructure entirely. A.O. Smith has invested in commercial heat pump product development and training (Smart Hybrid, Commercial Electric CHP). Rheem also offers heat pump products, but A.O. Smith's training curriculum appears more developed for commercial heat pump applications. This is relevant for facilities where venting modification costs approach or exceed the cost of electrical upgrades for heat pump alternatives.

**Data reference:** A.O. Smith University offers dedicated Commercial Heat Pump Water Heater courses (12 virtual classes in fall 2025).

## 10. Limitations of This Report

1. **Price and installed cost data are not publicly disclosed** for either manufacturer across commercial product lines. The omission of price from the scoring matrix is material. Actual project costs vary by region, installation complexity, and channel partner pricing.
2. **Reliability data is manufacturer-sourced or competitor-sourced.** Neither Rheem nor A.O. Smith publishes warranty claim rates or field failure data. The A.O. Smith University comparison document is competitor-produced and should be weighted accordingly.
3. **The Supreme Court petition creates regulatory uncertainty.** If the Court grants certiorari and stays the rule, or if DOE grants the AGA/APGA/NPGA petition for a 2030 compliance date, the entire competitive landscape changes. This report reflects the regulatory environment as of October 6, 2026.
4. **Tampa Bay-specific performance data is limited.** Hard water effects, humidity, and local code variations affect both brands. Neither manufacturer publishes region-specific reliability data for Hillsborough, Pinellas, or Pasco counties.
5. **Tankless vs. storage comparison is not apples-to-apples.** Rheem's tankless strength and A.O. Smith's storage strength reflect different application profiles. The "split decision" verdict reflects this structural difference.
6. **Manufacturer bias disclosure.** Product claims from Rheem.com and Hotwater.com are primary-source manufacturer positioning. Cross-verification against DOE standards and third-party regulatory documents was performed where possible, but brand-specific marketing claims are inherently biased.

## 12. FAQ

**Q1: What is the DOE commercial water heater standard effective October 6, 2026?**
A: The DOE final rule requires commercial gas storage water heaters to achieve 95% minimum thermal efficiency (up from 80%) and commercial gas instantaneous water heaters to achieve 96% (up from 80%). Residential-duty commercial gas storage must meet 0.9297 UEF. These standards eliminate non-condensing gas technology.

**Q2: Can contractors still install non-condensing water heaters after October 6, 2026?**
A: The DOE will not seek civil penalties for violations regarding equipment manufactured between October 6, 2026 and October 6, 2027. However, manufacturers cannot produce non-condensing units after October 6, 2026. The practical effect is a finite inventory window, not a compliance holiday.

**Q3: How much does venting modification cost for a condensing water heater retrofit?**
A: DOE installation cost tables show power-vent replacement scenarios totaling approximately $725 for vertical vent retrofit versus $420 for standard atmospheric replacement, with additional costs for electrical outlets ($310) and horizontal venting. Actual costs vary by building type and vent run complexity.

**Q4: Which brand has more commercial condensing experience, Rheem or A.O. Smith?**
A: A.O. Smith launched its first condensing commercial water heater (BFC Cyclone) in 1998 and marked the Cyclone platform's 30th anniversary in 2026. Rheem's commercial condensing storage platform has a shorter documented history, though Rheem's tankless condensing line (IKONIC) is newer and highly capable.

**Q5: What thermal efficiency do Rheem and A.O. Smith condensing water heaters achieve?**
A: Rheem's IKONIC tankless line achieves 98% thermal efficiency. A.O. Smith's Cyclone models range from 94% to 98% depending on model, with the XP Plus achieving 98% and the Cyclone XL achieving 97%.

**Q6: Is the DOE commercial water heater rule being challenged in court?**
A: Yes. The American Gas Association, American Public Gas Association, and National Propane Gas Association filed a petition for writ of certiorari (No. 25-879) with the U.S. Supreme Court, requesting review of the D.C. Circuit decision upholding the rule. The associations have also petitioned DOE to extend the compliance date to January 1, 2030.

**Q7: What warranty does Rheem offer on commercial water heaters?**
A: Rheem's commercial electric Eclipse series carries a 10-year limited tank warranty. The ELD66-D light-service commercial electric offers a 6-year limited tank warranty. Commercial gas warranty terms vary by model and are not uniformly disclosed in public product literature.

**Q8: What warranty does A.O. Smith offer on commercial water heaters?**
A: A.O. Smith's Cyclone HE carries a 6-year limited tank / 3-year limited parts warranty. Cyclone Xi carries 3-year tank / 1-year parts. Cyclone XL carries 5-year tank / 1-year parts. The XP Plus carries a 5-year heat exchanger limited warranty.
